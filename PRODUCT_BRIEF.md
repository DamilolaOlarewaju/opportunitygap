# OpportunityGap

## Who and what
OpportunityGap helps a MIVA student preparing for an internship understand how a specific role's requirements relate to their CV evidence, understand what is supported or missing, and choose a practical skill or project to develop next. The idea comes from the project owner’s wish to understand how their CV fits a role and what to add to their GitHub or CV.

## Problem and evidence
In four initial survey responses, three people were looking for internships and three reported avoiding an application because they were unsure whether their skills or projects were enough. Their selected challenges were identifying missing skills, knowing what to learn first, proving skills, and building a portfolio (one response each). All four use ChatGPT/AI when missing a skill. Written answers mentioned employer expectations, degree/experience requirements, confidence, CV preparation and age.

Source: From Learning to Opportunity — MIVA Student Research (Responses).xlsx, Form Responses 1, four responses supplied on 7 October 2026. One respondent did not understand the follow-up question. This is preliminary, self-reported evidence, not representative research or proof of product demand. No additional interviews are required for this prototype.

## First experience
The student pastes their CV text and a specific job or internship description, with optional extra project details. The app produces a requirement-by-requirement comparison, showing the supporting input or what remains unknown. It distinguishes learning gaps from degree, experience and other eligibility requirements. It proposes one achievable next action with a deliverable and a way to check completion. It also suggests CV wording based only on existing evidence, and explains what a completed project could contribute to GitHub and a future CV update.

## Memorable demo moment
A student can see exactly which requirement a suggested project addresses and what evidence that project could add to their portfolio. This is a proposed benefit to test, not an established outcome.

## Boundaries
The prototype will not predict hiring success, certify skills, make recruitment decisions, invent credentials, auto-apply, scrape job sites, or claim that a learning task removes degree or experience requirements. PDF upload, automatic GitHub analysis, accounts, notifications, payments and a course marketplace are outside the first version.

## Assumptions and success
Assumptions: students can supply readable job text and readable CV text; an evidence-linked next step is more useful than generic AI advice. Test these assumptions with the working app. Success for the build means the complete input-to-result flow works, every assessment is traceable, missing evidence stays unknown, and a clearly labelled sample demo works without a live AI service.
